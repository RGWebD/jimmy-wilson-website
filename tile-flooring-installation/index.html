Scope the work before choosing shortcuts

A straight layout begins with the room—not the wall

Walls and openings are not always square, and a rushed starting line can leave narrow cuts in the most visible place. JW reviews focal points, doorways and transitions before setting the layout. Tile size and pattern also influence cuts, joint placement and how variations in the surface will show.

Choose for the way the space is used

The substrate must be ready for a rigid finish

Tile does not bend with an unstable surface. Loose areas, visible cracks, movement and unevenness need to be understood before installation. JW identifies accessible preparation concerns during the estimate and scopes the work around the selected tile and setting materials, without promising that a cosmetic repair will stop structural movement.

No fixed price, cure time, warranty or performance claim is made on this page. Those details belong in the written scope for the selected system and actual site conditions.

Questions before an estimate

Useful answers before the site visit.

These answers are planning guidance, not a diagnosis of a surface that has not been inspected.

What is the difference between porcelain and ceramic tile?

They differ in composition and performance characteristics. The product rating, room conditions and intended use matter more than the label alone when choosing tile.

Can tile be installed over existing tile?

It may be possible when the existing surface is secure, clean and compatible, but height and underlying problems still matter. JW will inspect before recommending that approach.

Will grout be completely stain-proof?

No grout should be treated as maintenance-free. Product selection, sealing requirements, cleanup and regular care all affect how it looks over time.

Can one cracked tile be replaced?

Often, if a suitable replacement exists and the surrounding installation is sound. The cause of the crack should be considered before treating it as an isolated cosmetic repair.
