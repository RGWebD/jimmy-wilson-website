Scope the work before choosing shortcuts

Preparation determines what the coating can do

Concrete may carry old paint, oil residue, cracks, patchwork or moisture-related symptoms. Those conditions affect adhesion and the amount of preparation a project needs. JW reviews the accessible slab and existing finish before recommending a path, with cleaning, removal and repairs separated from the decorative coating itself.

Choose a finish for the space—not just the sample

Color and texture matter, but so do traffic, cleaning, wet use and the amount of downtime the property can accept. Questions before an estimate

Useful answers before the site visit.

These answers are planning guidance, not a diagnosis of a surface that has not been inspected.

Is every painted concrete floor an epoxy floor?

No. Concrete can be finished with several coating types. JW first identifies the existing surface and the intended use before discussing an appropriate system.

Can an existing coating be covered?

Sometimes, but not automatically. Its bond, compatibility and condition need to be assessed. Loose or incompatible material may need removal before recoating.

Can cracks be made invisible?

Repairs can reduce the appearance of many cracks, but concrete can continue to move. JW will explain what preparation can address and what may remain visible or return.

How long before the floor can be used?

That depends on the products, temperature, humidity and type of traffic. JW will provide the applicable access and cure guidance for the system selected for the job.
