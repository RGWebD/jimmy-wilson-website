Scope the work before choosing shortcuts

Compatibility comes before shine

Concrete, epoxy, resilient flooring and wood products do not share one universal clear coat. Existing wax, oil, cleaner residue or an unknown coating can interfere with a new layer. JW identifies the surface as far as practical and discusses testing or product limitations before the full application is approved.

Choose for the way the space is used

Set expectations for color, sheen and traction

Clear products can deepen color, change sheen or make wear patterns more noticeable. Added texture may affect cleaning, while a smoother finish can behave differently when wet. JW reviews an available finish direction in the context of the room and explains that maintenance cycles depend on traffic, exposure and the product used.

Questions before an estimate

Useful answers before the site visit.

These answers are planning guidance, not a diagnosis of a surface that has not been inspected.

Can a clear coat go over any floor?

No. Compatibility, adhesion, current condition and the intended use all matter. The existing surface needs to be identified and assessed first.

Will a clear coat hide scratches?

It may change how minor wear looks, but it can also highlight damage. Deeper defects generally need separate preparation or repair.

How often does a floor need resealing?

There is no single interval. Traffic, cleaning, sunlight, moisture and the product used affect maintenance timing.

Can a sealer change the color?

Yes. Some products deepen or alter the appearance of the existing surface. The likely result should be discussed before full application.
