Scope the work before choosing shortcuts

Plan the floor as a complete room system

Flooring affects doors, baseboards, appliances, transitions and the height where one room meets the next. A useful plan accounts for those details before material is ordered. JW reviews the layout and visible surface condition, then identifies preparation or repair work that should be priced separately instead of appearing as a surprise during installation.

What the estimate should address

Four parts of a complete service conversation.

Specific materials and preparation depend on the property, existing surface and manufacturer requirements.

01

Room and transition review

Measure the work area and note doorways, adjoining floors, trim and appliance clearances.

02

Existing-floor assessment

Look for loose material, uneven areas and visible damage that may affect the new finish.

03

Preparation scope

Separate removal, cleaning, patching, leveling or other needed preparation in the estimate.

04

Finish details

Plan transitions, edge details and trim so the completed rooms connect cleanly.

Choose for the way the space is used

Match the material to daily use

A busy entry, a rental bedroom and a moisture-prone slab do not ask the same thing from a floor. Material selection should consider traffic, pets, cleaning, direct sun, room use and the manufacturer’s requirements. JW can talk through LVP, tile, laminate, hardwood and coated surfaces, then scope the installation around the chosen product and the actual site conditions.

No fixed price, cure time, warranty or performance claim is made on this page. Those details belong in the written scope for the selected system and actual site conditions.

Questions before an estimate

Useful answers before the site visit.

These answers are planning guidance, not a diagnosis of a surface that has not been inspected.

Which flooring materials can JW discuss for my project?

JW scopes projects involving LVP, LVT, tile, laminate, hardwood and floor coatings. The right option depends on the room, existing surface and the finish you want.

Can the old flooring be included in the scope?

Yes. Existing-floor removal and disposal can be discussed during the estimate and should be listed clearly when included.

What happens if the surface is uneven or damaged?

JW will identify visible preparation or repair needs during the site review. The appropriate correction depends on the material, severity and conditions found after removal.

Can painting and flooring be planned together?

Yes. Coordinating both trades can reduce scheduling conflicts around trim, baseboards and room access. The order is set after the actual scope is known.
