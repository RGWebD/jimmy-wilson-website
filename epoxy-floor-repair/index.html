Scope the work before choosing shortcuts

A patch is only as sound as the coating around it

If the surrounding finish is firmly bonded, an isolated repair may be practical. If failure is widespread or continues beyond the visible edge, covering one spot can leave the larger problem in place. JW checks the pattern, prior products when known and current use of the floor before defining a repair boundary.

Choose for the way the space is used

Expect appearance differences in local repairs

Age, wear, sunlight and product availability affect how closely a repaired section can match. Decorative flake can help blend some transitions, while solid or metallic finishes may show a repair more clearly. JW explains those visual tradeoffs before the project is approved.

Questions before an estimate

Useful answers before the site visit.

These answers are planning guidance, not a diagnosis of a surface that has not been inspected.

Can peeling epoxy be painted over?

Loose or failing material should not simply be buried. The soundness and compatibility of the remaining finish must be considered first.

Will a spot repair be invisible?

Not necessarily. Existing wear, color variation and product changes can leave a visible transition. JW will discuss the expected appearance before work.

What causes bubbles or blisters?

Several conditions can contribute, including trapped air, contamination, moisture or application factors. The visible symptom alone may not prove one cause.

When is full removal more practical?

Broad loss of adhesion, incompatible layers or repeated repairs can make a larger reset more sensible. The recommendation depends on the inspected floor.
